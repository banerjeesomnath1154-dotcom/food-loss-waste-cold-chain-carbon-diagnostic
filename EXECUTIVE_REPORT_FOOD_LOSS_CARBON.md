# 🥦 Executive Brief: Post-Harvest Food Loss & Waste (FLW) & Scope 3 Carbon Audit

## 📌 Executive Summary
Post-harvest food loss across India's agricultural supply chains causes severe dual financial and environmental losses. Across **1,000 tracked perishable shipment logs** representing **22,326.00 Tonnes** of fresh produce (*Grapes, Alphonso Mangoes, Bananas, Tomatoes, Red Chilli, and Red Onions*), physical spoilage accounts for **2,659.21 Tonnes (11.91% physical loss rate)**. 

This post-harvest degradation results in **₹26.59 Crores (₹265.90 Million)** in direct gross margin write-offs and generates **4,382.09 Tonnes of embodied Scope 3 CO₂e greenhouse gas emissions** wasted in transit and storage.

### Key Thermal Degradation Observations:
1. **First-Mile Thermal Stress:** Shipments lacking first-mile reefer pre-cooling (ambient temperatures > 35°C) experience **15.06% spoilage**, generating 39.4% of all supply chain carbon emissions.
2. **Cold Storage Resilience:** Once produce enters cold storage operating at target temperatures (2°C–8°C), loss drops to **9.26%**, proving that rapid temperature management preserves physical tonnage and financial margins.
